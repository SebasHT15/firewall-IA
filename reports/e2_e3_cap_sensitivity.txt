1. THE MECHANISM — WHY GROUP CAPS DO NOT CONTROL ROW DOMINANCE
==============================================================================
The question that motivated this analysis: 1,000 logical groups produce 6,347
rows for SQL Injection but 1,004 for Path Traversal. Measured cause:

  RENDERED ROWS PER LOGICAL GROUP, cap 1000 policy A:

    CATEGORY                  GROUPS   ROWS  from PATT  from CSIC   ROWS/GROUP
    ----------------------   -------  -----  ---------  ---------  -----------
    CRLF Injection                21    511         23        488        24.33
    SQL Injection              1,000  6,347      1,720      4,627         6.35
    XSS Injection              1,000  2,084      1,408        676         2.08
    XPath Injection               24     40         40          0         1.67
    Command Injection            453    741        632        109         1.64
    XXE Injection                 98    143        143          0         1.46
    Cross-Site Request Forgery    31     46         46          0         1.48
    File Inclusion             1,000  1,039      1,039          0         1.04
    Directory Traversal        1,000  1,004      1,004          0         1.00
    HTTP Parameter Pollution      20     20         20          0         1.00
    Request Smuggling              7      7          7          0         1.00

  Every category whose ratio exceeds ~2.0 is a category where CSIC contributes.
  Categories with no CSIC contribution sit at 1.0-1.7 rows per group.

  ROOT CAUSE: a CSIC group is a request SHAPE that collapsed many original
  records (41,900 records -> 8,519 groups). Every record in the group still
  renders. So one CSIC group can emit hundreds of rows, while one
  PayloadsAllTheThings group emits 1 row plus at most 1 augmented variant.

  CONSEQUENCE: capping LOGICAL GROUPS cannot control RENDERED-ROW dominance
  wherever CSIC contributes. This is why policy A fails (section 3).

  SECOND-ORDER FINDING — CRLF is a diversity illusion.
    CRLF holds 3.0-5.9% of BLOCK rows across scenarios, which looks adequate.
    But it has only 21 unique logical payloads; 488 of its 511 rows come from
    CSIC groups. Row count OVERSTATES its diversity by more than an order of
    magnitude. CRLF is on the INSUFFICIENT DATA list and must stay there
    regardless of how healthy its row share looks.

  cap1000_A is the CURRENT LIVE CANDIDATE (verified bit-identical).
  ALLOW/BLOCK is exactly 1:1 in every scenario (method-stratified balancing).

  ENTROPY — what it means here.
    Shannon entropy H = -SUM p_i * log2(p_i) over the 19 BLOCK categories'
    share of BLOCK rows. Maximum is log2(19) = 4.248 bits, reached only if all
    19 categories were equally represented.
    EFF.CAT = 2^H, the "effective number of equally-represented categories" —
    the most interpretable figure. cap1000_A scores 6.36, meaning its 19
    categories carry as much diversity as ~6 equally-sized ones.
    EVEN = H / log2(19), normalised to [0,1].
    These are DESCRIPTIVE. Higher is not automatically better: perfectly equal
    representation is explicitly NOT the objective.

3. POLICY A CANNOT CONTROL DOMINANCE — AND TIGHTENING IT MAKES IT WORSE
==============================================================================
  Largest attack-category share of BLOCK, policy A:
      cap 1000  47.3%      cap 2000  39.3%
      cap 1250  46.1%      cap 2500  36.5%
      cap 1500  43.7%      uncapped  38.1%

  Under policy A the largest category is SQL Injection in every scenario, and
  it never falls below 36.5%.

  The relationship is INVERTED relative to intuition: a TIGHTER cap produces
  MORE dominance. Cause — policy A only shrinks the PayloadsAllTheThings side.
  CSIC's ~4,600 SQL rows are untouched, so tightening the cap removes the
  traffic that was diluting them. At cap 1000 the SQL row count is 6,347, of
  which only 1,720 came from PayloadsAllTheThings.

  This is the central negative result of the analysis: the D17 lever, as
  currently defined, does not act on the quantity it was intended to control.

==============================================================================
4. POLICY B — DIRECT CONTROL, PAID FOR IN SIZE AND SOURCE DIVERSITY
==============================================================================
  Largest share, policy B:
      cap 1000  26.0%      cap 2000  31.4%
      cap 1250  27.7%      cap 2500  32.2%
      cap 1500  28.4%

  Policy B behaves monotonically and as intended. The cost is twofold:

  (a) SIZE. At the same cap, B is 25-35% smaller than A, because CSIC BLOCK
      groups are dropped and the 1:1 method-stratified balance then trims the
      ALLOW side to match.

  (b) SOURCE DIVERSITY. B discards REAL captured traffic. At cap 2000 the SQL
      CSIC contribution falls from 4,627 rows to 3,051. That is a genuine loss:
      CSIC is the only non-synthetic attack traffic in the dataset. The
      analysis does not treat this as free.

  What B does NOT do: it does not fix rows-per-group. CRLF stays at 24.33
  rows/group in every B scenario, because 21 + its CSIC groups never reaches
  any tested cap.

  HOW TO CHOOSE
    The two differ by 0.8 points of largest-category share and 5,452 rows.
    If the binding constraint is that SQL Injection must not dominate a small
    dataset, take Candidate 1. If the binding constraint is that 26,820 rows is
    already thin for a 1.1B model, take Candidate 2 — it buys 28% more data for
    almost no extra dominance.

    NOT RECOMMENDED: cap1000_B. It has the best balance figures of any scenario
    (26.0% largest, 9.60 effective categories) but only 17,366 rows — a 35% cut
    against a candidate already flagged as small. Optimising evenness to that
    point is exactly the "perfectly equal distribution" objective this analysis
    was told not to pursue.

    ALSO NOT RECOMMENDED: staying at cap1000_A. Section 5 shows it is worse
    than both candidates on size and on every dominance measure at once.

==============================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS OF THIS ANALYSIS
==============================================================================
  1. No model was trained. Every figure is a DATASET property. Whether lower
     category dominance improves per-category recall is a hypothesis this
     analysis cannot test.
  2. Entropy/evenness are descriptive summaries, not objectives.
  3. Only uniform per-category caps were tested. Per-category budgets, or a cap
     on RENDERED ROWS rather than logical groups, were not explored and might
     dominate both candidates. A row-level cap follows directly from section 1
     and is the obvious next lever if neither candidate satisfies.
  4. Policy B drops whole CSIC groups. Sub-sampling records WITHIN a CSIC group
     would reduce rows while retaining shape diversity, and was not tested.
  5. The augmentation multiplier (1 per group) was held constant throughout.
  6. Scarcity is untouched by any scenario (section 6).
