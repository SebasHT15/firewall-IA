  WHY THIS IS NOT THE HEADLINE
    An always-BLOCK classifier scores 80.7% on this suite. The 26 ALLOW cases
    cannot support a false-positive-rate claim: the 23.08% FPR here rests on 6
    errors out of 26 items, whereas the formal split measures 0.06% on 3,103
    benign rows. The suite's adversarial cases also reuse transform families
    present in training augmentation, so they are not evidence of evasion
    resistance.

    The divergence between 23.08% (n=26) and 0.06% (n=3,103) is exactly the
    reason D19 demoted this suite. The 6 legacy false positives are worth
    inspecting qualitatively as edge cases; they are not a rate.

11. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
==============================================================================
  1. Single training run, single seed. No variance estimate, no confidence
     intervals. Point estimates only.
  2. Evasion resistance UNMEASURED by design (D15). Held-out transform
     families generate nothing in this dataset.
  3. Benign population is synthetic + CSIC 2010. A 0.06% FPR here does not
     establish a low FPR on live production traffic.
  4. CSIC BLOCK labels come from an 11-rule keyword heuristic (audit F6). The
     CSIC-derived portion of every per-category result inherits that
     circularity, and SQL injection is the most affected category.
  5. Reason matching is exact-after-normalisation, deliberately strict. Reason
     accuracy is not comparable to loosely-scored figures elsewhere.
  6. 11 categories cannot support robust per-category conclusions; 1 is
     unevaluable entirely.
  7. Latency is model-side only, on laptop-class hardware, at batch size 1.
  8. eval_loss minimum at step 2200 with a rise by 3144 indicates mild
     overfitting; the best checkpoint was selected automatically, but a
     shorter schedule was not explored and must not be inferred as better
     without an experiment.

==============================================================================
12. OBSERVATIONS
==============================================================================
  1. The first interpretable baseline exists: 98.49% accuracy with a 0.06%
     false-positive rate on 3,103 benign held-out rows, on a dataset where no
     incidental envelope feature exceeds 51.16% against a 50.00% majority.

  2. The error profile is strongly asymmetric — 92 false negatives against 2
     false positives. For an inline gateway this is the safer direction
     operationally (benign traffic is rarely broken), but it means roughly 3%
     of attacks pass. Whether that trade is acceptable is a project decision,
     not a measurement.

  3. False negatives are highly concentrated: 80.4% in SQL injection and
     18.5% in Command injection. Two categories account for 98.9% of all
     missed attacks. This is a specific, actionable finding for future work.

  4. Binary detection and reason naming are largely decoupled — several
     categories show 100% binary recall with imperfect reason accuracy. The
     E5 separation was necessary; a combined metric would have hidden this.

  5. Zero invalid outputs across 6,206 generations, and native EOS terminated
     100% of sampled generations at 9-13 tokens. The E4 output contract holds
     under a trained model.

  6. Model-side P95 (270.8 ms) already exceeds the whole end-to-end D3 budget
     (200 ms). Quantization work in M2 is not an optimization nicety; it is on
     the critical path to a viable inline gateway.

  NO THESIS CONCLUSIONS ARE DRAWN HERE. These are measurements under stated
  conditions, with the limitations in section 11 attached.
